A prototype AI chatbot takes **1–2 weeks**. A production assistant that answers from your own content using retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) takes **4–6 weeks**. If it also does things in one or two systems, like booking appointments or updating a CRM, plan on **8–10 weeks**. Acting across several systems makes it an AI agent: **12+ weeks**. Fine-tuning adds **2–4 weeks**, and most business chatbots don't need it.

Where you land depends on the state of your content, how many systems the bot touches, and how much proof you need that it won't mislead a customer. Channels and compliance add calendar time on top, mostly waiting on someone else. If you're budgeting too, see [what an AI chatbot costs](/blog/cost-to-build-ai-chatbot).

## What are the realistic timeline tiers?

Three tiers cover chatbots proper, and our [AI chatbot development service](/services/ai-chatbot-development) scopes against them.

| Tier | Timeline | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scoped prototype | 1–2 weeks | A chat interface over one model and a slice of your real content, enough to test answer quality on your own questions before you commit budget |
| Production RAG assistant | 4–6 weeks | Retrieval over your full knowledge base with citations, refusal behaviour, analytics, human handoff, one channel and a scored evaluation set |
| Takes actions in one or two systems | 8–10 weeks | All of the above, plus actions such as booking, creating tickets or writing to a CRM, with a confirmation step, role-based access and audit logging |

*The timelines we quote, assuming API access and source content exist on day one. Often they don't.*

A fourth tier starts when the bot acts across several systems. That's an agent, and **12+ weeks**. Our guide to [how long AI agent development takes](/blog/how-long-to-build-ai-agent) breaks it down: about 2 weeks for an agent prototype, 6–10 for one production workflow, 12+ for multi-system work.

## How long does a chatbot take for your scenario?

Anywhere from one week to twelve-plus. The last column decides the date, and it's rarely code.

| Scenario | Typical timeline | What sets the pace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FAQ bot over one help centre | 1–2 weeks to a prototype, about 4 to production | Whether the help centre contradicts itself |
| Support bot that hands off to your helpdesk | 5–6 weeks | Agreeing when it hands off, what it passes on, and what happens out of hours |
| Internal knowledge assistant over Notion or Google Drive that respects permissions | About 6 weeks, longer if sharing settings are a mess | Making sure the bot never quotes a page the person asking couldn't open |
| WhatsApp sales or support bot that answers questions | 5–6 weeks, with Meta's reviews running in parallel | Business verification, display name and template approval |
| Bot that books appointments or updates a CRM | 8–10 weeks | Write access, confirmation before anything changes, and testing actions as well as answers |
| Assistant that acts across several systems | 12+ weeks | It's an agent, and each system's access approval runs on its own calendar |

Torn between two rows? Start with the smaller one.

## Where do the weeks actually go? A worked 5-week plan

Mostly into making answers trustworthy. Hooking up the model is the quick bit. Here's the plan for a production support assistant launching as a website widget.

| Week | Work | What you see by Friday |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Discovery and data audit: real questions from tickets and chat logs, where answers live, refusal and handoff rules. File credential and channel requests. | A scoped question list, an owner per source, content conflicts to fix, a draft evaluation set |
| 2 | Retrieval pipeline: ingest, chunk and index the content so every answer cites its source | An internal test bot answering from your documents, with citations |
| 3 | Answer quality: score the evaluation set, tune retrieval and prompts, fix content at source | A score on real questions, and what it still gets wrong and why |
| 4 | Integration and guardrails: widget, AI disclosure, refusal rules, rate limiting, analytics, helpdesk handoff | The bot on staging, handing a test conversation and transcript to a human agent |
| 5 | Hardening and soft launch: load tests, a fallback for model outages, retention settings, release to a share of visitors | A live bot for some users, a dashboard of unanswered questions and handoffs, a go or no-go on full rollout |

Weeks one and three decide whether the plan holds.

Week one is where contradictory content turns up. Your pricing page says one thing, an old help article another, and the bot will happily quote either. That's the most common cause of delay, and no engineering fixes it. Someone on your side has to pick the true version.

## How long does it take to train a chatbot?

For most business chatbots, no time at all. They're never trained.

RAG looks your content up when the question arrives, so updating the bot means re-indexing documents. Minutes. A no-code builder that offers to "train" on your website or files is indexing too, under a friendlier word.

Real fine-tuning (adjusting a model's weights on your own examples) adds **2–4 weeks** for a labelled dataset, training, and evaluation against the stock model. It helps with a consistent tone or a rigid output format.

Don't fine-tune to fix wrong answers. Fine-tuning teaches style. Retrieval supplies the truth. Try it anyway and you usually get a model that's confidently wrong in a more on-brand voice. Our [comparison of prompting, fine-tuning and RAG](/blog/prompt-engineering-vs-fine-tuning-vs-rag) explains when each applies. If the goal is "the bot should know our products, policies and docs", you need retrieval and zero training time.

## How much time does each channel add?

Little engineering, sometimes a lot of calendar. Most channels put someone else's review between you and launch.

I worked as a full-stack engineer on an [omnichannel messaging SaaS](/work/omnichannel-messaging-saas) (not a CodeBaxh-led build) that put Messenger, Instagram, SMS, web chat and Google Business Messages in one inbox. Five channels, five sets of rules. It's why we launch bots on one first.

### Web widget

Start here. You control the release, nobody else reviews it, and you can soft-launch to a slice of visitors. Fastest by a distance, and my pick for almost any first launch.

### Slack

An internal bot needs no Marketplace listing, but workspace owners can switch on [Only allow pre-approved apps](https://slack.com/help/articles/222386767-Manage-app-approval-for-your-workspace), and then your install waits for their approval. Slack gives no duration, so ask early.

Listing only matters if you sell the bot to other companies. Slack's [review guide](https://docs.slack.dev/slack-marketplace/slack-marketplace-review-guide) requires 10 or more active workspace installs, quotes up to 10 business days for preliminary feedback and up to 10 weeks for functional review of a new app, and won't expedite. Ten weeks. Plan around it.

### Microsoft Teams

An internal bot is submitted to the Teams admin center and stays blocked until an admin [reviews and publishes it](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/submit-approve-custom-apps). Microsoft gives no duration either, so get that admin's name at kickoff, not in week five.

The public Teams Store adds Partner Center validation. Microsoft [aims](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/platform/concepts/deploy-and-publish/appsource/publish) to return a test report within 24 working hours, then you fix and resubmit until approved.

### WhatsApp Business Platform

Nothing that slows a WhatsApp bot down is engineering. It's Meta's process.

A user's message [opens a 24-hour window](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/messages/send-messages/) that resets each time they write. Inside it the bot replies freely. After it closes, only pre-approved templates can be sent, so reminders and follow-ups need one. Meta's [template review](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/templates/template-review/) can take up to 24 hours per decision, and rejections get edited and resubmitted, or appealed.

Then there are messaging limits. A new business portfolio can message 250 unique users per moving 24 hours outside a service window, and [verifying your business](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/messaging-limits) is one route to 2,000. Inbound-only bots barely notice. Bots that send reminders or offers do. Your name only shows in chat headers after [display name verification](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/display-names), which runs when you reach a higher messaging limit.

Before you build anything, have legal read section 4.7 of Meta's [WhatsApp Business Platform terms](https://www.facebook.com/legal/Meta-Terms-for-WhatsApp-Business-Platform). It bars AI providers when AI is the primary, "rather than incidental or ancillary", functionality. Supporting your own customers differs from offering a general-purpose assistant, but Meta decides.

Meta's developer documentation gives no duration for business verification. Start it in week one.

## Which compliance steps affect the timeline?

Mostly decisions, and they only cost weeks when they start late.

### EU AI Act: say it is an AI

Article 50 of the AI Act has [applied since 2 August 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act). The European Commission's guidance names chatbots: people must be told clearly, from the start of the first interaction, that they're dealing with an AI system, and the exception for obvious cases is to be read restrictively. Building the disclosure takes hours. Signing off the wording takes longer.

### GDPR: a processor contract and a retention period

A model provider processing customer messages for you must be [bound by a contract](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme-data-protection-guide/data-controller-data-processor_en) covering your instructions, sub-processors, and deletion or return of data. Anthropic's [Data Processing Addendum](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/data-processing-addendum), for example, forms part of its Commercial Terms and names Anthropic as your processor.

Then set retention. The EDPB expects an [internal retention policy for each purpose](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme-data-protection-guide/data-protection-basics_en), so decide how long transcripts live, with you and the provider. OpenAI [keeps](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data) API abuse-monitoring logs for up to 30 days by default, and zero data retention needs its prior approval. Request it in week one.

### HIPAA: a BAA before patient data flows

Protected health information needs a business associate agreement (BAA) first. Anthropic [offers a BAA](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/8114513-business-associate-agreements-baa-for-commercial-customers) for HIPAA-ready services, including its first-party API, but it isn't on by default and excludes some API features. OpenAI's data-controls page (linked above) refers to an executed OpenAI Business Associate and Healthcare Addendum and lists features a BAA doesn't cover, web search among them. Confirm coverage during discovery, because exclusions shape the architecture. Week four is a bad time to find one.

## Do multilingual chatbots take longer?

Yes, in evaluation. Models handle many languages, but quality you've proven in one doesn't transfer to the next. Each language needs its own real questions with known-correct answers, scored by a fluent reviewer. A bot that scores well in English can still retrieve the wrong passage for a Spanish question over English-only content.

Check your agents can take handoffs in each language, then launch in one and add the rest one at a time.

## How do human handoff and helpdesk integration fit in?

Handoff belongs in the 4–6-week tier. The time goes on agreeing rules, so settle these before week four:

- **When it hands off.** The user asks for a person, there's no grounded answer, the topic is sensitive (complaints, refunds, legal or medical), or the conversation loops.
- **What it passes on.** The transcript and details collected, so nobody repeats themselves.
- **Who picks it up.** The right queue. Outside staffed hours, a ticket plus an honest message about response times.
- **Who talks next.** Once a person takes over, the bot goes quiet. (Obvious, but write it down.)

If your helpdesk has an API, passing a conversation into its queue is days of work. Changing ticket fields, issuing refunds or updating orders is the bot taking actions, and that's the 8–10-week tier.

## What makes a chatbot build take longer?

Contradictory content, above all (see week one). Cleaning it up adds 1–2 weeks. Waiting on API access comes next, because credentials and enterprise approval loops often take longer than the engineering.

Then permissions. Inconsistent sharing settings turn a retrieval job into a permissions audit. I'd enforce who-sees-what in the data layer, not in a prompt. Postgres row-level security does exactly that, and it was in the stack on a K-12 AI tutoring service I worked on this autumn. That work is merged and live.

After those: several channels at once (you wait for the slowest review), and nobody who can define "correct", which means no evaluation set and no honest way to call the bot ready. Legal or security sign-off, common in fintech and health, adds weeks that have nothing to do with code.

## How do you compress the timeline?

Pick **one channel**, **one job** (support Q&A, not support plus sales plus onboarding) and **existing content** that already answers the questions. Widening later is cheap. Starting wide is how six weeks becomes twelve.

Start the outside processes on day one: Meta verification, the Teams admin approval, a provider DPA or BAA. None of them needs finished code.

Model choice barely moves the schedule. In a well-built pipeline, switching providers is a configuration change, so don't lose a fortnight comparing them.

## What should you prepare before kickoff?

Each item removes a wait.

1. **Real questions** from a recent export of tickets or chat logs.
2. **Correct answers to the top questions**, to seed the evaluation set.
3. **A source map.** Where answers live, who owns each source, and which wins when two disagree.
4. **A refusal and handoff list.** Topics the bot must never answer, and who takes each.
5. **Credentials and an admin contact** for every system the bot touches.
6. **Channel accounts.** Meta verification under way, and the Slack owner or Teams admin who'll approve the app.
7. **Compliance decisions.** The provider's DPA, transcript retention, disclosure wording, and a BAA for health data.
8. **A product owner who answers within a day.** A decision that waits a week costs a week.
9. **A definition of ready.** The evaluation score that justifies launch.

## Should you build or buy?

Buy if your helpdesk has a built-in AI agent and your help centre answers most questions. Nothing we build will be faster. Intercom [says](https://www.intercom.com/pricing) Fin can be set up on your current helpdesk in under an hour. At the time of writing (November 2026), Fin costs $0.99 per outcome, counted when a customer confirms a resolution, stops asking for help, or Fin completes a workflow (handoffs included). [Zendesk AI agents](https://www.zendesk.com/pricing/) come with its Suite and Support plans and are billed per automated resolution. Allow a few days for content, handoff rules and testing.

Build when answers depend on systems the product can't reach, or the bot must act in your own tools. Same if you need an unsupported channel or language, or usage fees at your volume would exceed running your own.

Below a few hundred conversations a month, a well-organised FAQ page may beat both. Unglamorous, but price it before you spend anything.

## What happens in the first 30 days after launch?

Real users ask what your evaluation set didn't. Our guide to [hypercare and post-launch support](/blog/hypercare-post-launch-support) covers how that month is run.

Review unanswered questions daily at first, along with low-confidence and handed-off conversations. Most fixes are content. Fix at source and re-index rather than patching the prompt, and schedule re-indexing (or trigger it on publish) so updates reach the bot without a developer.

## Frequently asked questions

### How fast can we have something to try?

One to two weeks, for a clickable prototype over a slice of your real content. It isn't production-ready, but it tells you whether answer quality is good enough to build on before you commit a full budget.

### Can the chatbot use our internal documents?

Yes. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) looks up relevant passages in your documents and answers from them with citations. For Notion or Google Drive, allow about six weeks, since retrieval must respect who can open what.

### Do we need to fine-tune a model?

Usually not. Fine-tuning changes tone and output format, while retrieval handles facts, so most business chatbots ship with a stock model. If a tone or format problem remains once retrieval works, fine-tuning adds two to four weeks.

### How long does it take to train a chatbot?

Usually no time at all. A RAG chatbot looks answers up in your indexed documents, so updating it means re-indexing, which takes minutes. Real fine-tuning adds two to four weeks, for tone or format rather than facts.

### What does an AI chatbot cost to run after launch?

Model API usage, hosting and keeping content indexed, plus per-message fees on channels such as WhatsApp. Budget time too, for the monthly review of unanswered questions that keeps answer quality improving.

### How long does it take to make a simple chatbot?

One to two weeks for a testable bot over one help centre (a chat interface, indexed content and refusal behaviour), and about four to put it in front of customers. Contradictory source content is what usually slows it down.

### Can you build a chatbot in a week?

As a prototype, yes: a working chat interface over a slice of your real documents. A week doesn't buy a scored evaluation set, guardrails, human handoff, load testing or analytics. Those separate a demo from a product, and they fill most of the 4-to-6-week timeline.

### What is a realistic chatbot development time for a small business?

Four to six weeks for a production support assistant, if your help content exists and you start with one channel. Below a few hundred conversations a month, price your helpdesk's built-in AI agent first. At that scale it often wins on cost.

### How long does it take to build a WhatsApp chatbot?

Five to six weeks for a bot that answers questions, eight to ten if it books appointments or updates records. Meta's business verification, display name review and template approval (up to 24 hours per template decision) set the pace, so start them in week one.

### How long does a no-code chatbot take?

Days, if your help content already answers what customers ask. A helpdesk's built-in AI agent indexes a help centre quickly, and Intercom says Fin can be set up in under an hour. Add a few days for handoff rules and testing.

### Does the EU AI Act affect our chatbot timeline?

Only slightly. Article 50 has applied since 2 August 2026, and the European Commission's guidance names chatbots: people must be told clearly, from the first interaction, that they're talking to an AI. Building that takes hours. Agreeing the wording takes longer, so settle it in discovery.
